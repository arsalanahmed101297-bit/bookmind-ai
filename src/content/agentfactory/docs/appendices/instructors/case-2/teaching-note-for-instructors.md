> **For instructors.** Students can read this page, but it works best if they discuss the case first.

## What the case is about

In [Case 1](../../../claude-and-chatgpt-2/case-1-nishas-ai-team/overview.md), Nisha managed two AI tools while she sat in front of them. In this case she owns an AI Worker that works at night. She writes a brief, builds a project with threads, schedules a nightly run, and decides what the worker can reach. On Thursday night, her client asks for the final numbers on time and for updates sent straight from the worker. One line of the bridge is not yet checked. The case ends before she decides.

The accounting is the setting, not the lesson. Students need no accounting knowledge. The lesson is about who does the work, who checks it, who sends it, and under whose name.

**Important:** the practical behind this case was run on November 17, 2026, in one sitting. Each nightly run was started by hand, and no person did Nisha's part. Exhibits 3, 5 and 8 hold the recorded results, and each one says where the story and the record differ. Tell students this at the start. Two things in the story are not from the record: Nisha's own time, and the run that stopped at a usage limit on Thursday, which follows Anthropic's docs.

## Learning objectives

After the discussion, students should be able to:

1. Explain what changes when work moves from "while I watch" to "while I sleep": the brief must stand alone, the record must live outside the desk, and the person checks results afterwards (Exhibits 2, 3 and 8).
2. Separate rules that are enforced by a setting or a missing permission from rules that are only written down (Exhibit 6).
3. Describe what several reviewers add, and what only the person with the client relationship can check (Exhibit 5).
4. Explain whose name a worker's actions appear under, and what a business would need to add (Exhibit 7 and the story).
5. Make and defend a decision under a deadline, with the risks named (core question 5).
6. Say what must be true before professional work is released, and who may release it (Exhibit 9).

## Before class

If students will do the lab, set this order: "A paid job" and "Building the AI Worker", then the lab, then "Two nights of work", "Thursday night" and the exhibits. The later pages show what Nisha found, so reading them first spoils the count of who caught what. The answer key is not in the learner download. The lab links to it at the end of Part 7, so ask students to open it only then. Then ask students to read the four story pages and skim the exhibits. Ask each student to choose one of Nisha's three drafts before class, and to write one sentence on why.

## Discussion plan (75 minutes)

| Time | Question | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 10 min | Core 1: What changed from Case 1? | Compare "while she watched" with "while she slept". Use Exhibits 3 and 8. |
| 10 to 25 min | Core 2: Enforced or only words? | Build the permission column of the board. Use Exhibit 6. |
| 25 to 40 min | Core 3: Who caught what? | Use Exhibit 5. Draw out what only Nisha could do. |
| 40 to 50 min | Core 4: Whose name? | Use the story and Exhibit 7. Link to identity and the business layer. |
| 50 to 70 min | Core 5: What should Nisha send tonight? | Use the decision layout and Exhibit 9. Take a vote, then ask the minority to argue. |
| 70 to 75 min | Close, with series question 12 | Containment or authority: which protected Mark, and which protected Nisha? |

For an 80-minute class, add series question 11 after core 3, for about 5 minutes.

## Board plan

Use three columns.

| What the worker did | What only Nisha did | What nothing decided |
|---|---|---|
| Prepared the bridge each night. Found the differences. Listed its assumptions. Flagged the rent figures and the inventory gap. | Wrote the brief. Chose what to connect. Filtered the second opinion. Checked lines against sources. Wrote to Sutlej. | What to send to Mark tonight. Whether the worker may email clients. |

### Decision layout for core 5

| Draft | Evidence for | Evidence against | Risk if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Say yes to both | On time. Pleases the client. Five more jobs possible. | Lease line unchecked. Future emails unread by her but sent under her name. | Wrong numbers to a board, from her address |
| 2. Keep the second signature | Every number sent is checked. | Gap in the bridge before the board meeting. | Losing the client |
| 3. Send everything, with a note | On time and honest. Both lease answers are shown. | The board may not read the note. | A footnote becomes the number |

## Notes on the core questions

**Core 1.** Students often say "she saved time". Push them: did she? Her own time rose (Exhibit 8 gives her time from the story, not from a measurement, but the design shows why: setup, morning checks, filtering, emails). The record adds a point. The threads and each run took only minutes, so the night did not buy speed. It bought work done without her. What she gained was work done while she was away, and a record. What she gave up was being present when each decision was made.

**Core 2.** Most students will list the rule "Only I send things to the client" as her main safeguard. Ask what would happen if she had connected her email. The rule would still be only words. The missing email app is what enforces it. Also point out the "only asked" rows in Exhibit 6: nothing stops a thread from saving a changed copy of the client's file. Also ask about the run that continued on Thursday: did it pick up Wednesday's two new points? It did not. Changes to instructions reach new threads, not threads already running, and the nightly run continues one thread. In the recorded practical, the run reported that nothing had changed. Version 2 shows both points only because Nisha then added them to the task's own instruction. Optional question 7 picks this up.

**Core 3.** Exhibit 5 is recorded, except its Nisha column. Start with the design. Different reviewers saw different things (the Who saw what figure in Exhibit 5): the area threads and the reviewer thread saw the client's files in the Library, but each area thread saw only its own part of the bridge. ChatGPT saw the rules and the bridge, but not the files. Only Nisha saw the emails with Sutlej and Mark and could write to the client. Finding a gap is not the same as closing it: the reviewer thread listed the inventory gap and the rent figures, but it could not say which figures were right. Only the person with the relationship could ask Sutlej which rent figure was right.

**Core 4.** Anything a scheduled task does through connectors appears as the person, on every plan. Ask: would Mark be able to tell what Nisha had read? Link to Claude Tag (series question 13) and to what a business adds: its own identity for the worker, approvals recorded in a system, and a lasting record.

**Core 5.** There is no right answer. Strong answers name what each draft protects and what it risks, and say what Nisha would tell Mark in one sentence. Weak answers choose by feeling. Push students who choose draft 1 on the nightly emails. Push students who choose draft 2 on the client relationship.

Then push every group through [Exhibit 9](../../../claude-and-chatgpt-2/case-2-nishas-first-client/exhibits.md#exhibit-9-before-anything-is-released), the acceptance checklist. The board is deciding whether to invest. Ask: is this bridge a preliminary scenario analysis or a signed-off conversion? The lease contract is open, and it could change the balance sheet as well as the profit line. The inventory difference is explained only by an email. Who has the US GAAP expertise and the authority to release a signed-off conversion? Nisha is still learning US GAAP. A strong answer labels whatever leaves as "Preliminary scenario analysis, not a signed-off conversion" and names who would have to sign before it is called final. This is a discussion of professional judgment, not accounting advice.

## Takeaways for the wrap-up

- A worker that runs while you sleep needs a brief a stranger could follow, and a record outside the desk.
- The safest rules are the ones a setting or a missing permission enforces. Written rules still matter, but they are not locks.
- More reviewers find more, but only the person who owns the client relationship can check some things.
- Through your connectors, the worker acts under your name. That is why the second signature matters most for anything that leaves the company.

## Running the class with non-accountants

Tell students at the start that they do not need to understand the accounting. Explain a bridge as "a list of adjustments from one rulebook to another". Treat each line of Exhibit 4 as "one rule difference with an amount". The lease line is enough to understand as "one of two answers, and only a contract she has not seen can say which". Keep the discussion on the decisions, not the standards.

## Notes on the series questions

- **11, where the work lived:** the Library and Nisha's email held the lasting record. Threads and their desks did not. Deleting a project removes its threads, memory and files. Push further: a Library is right for drafts and learning, but it is not a governed business record. A business AI Worker keeps progress, approvals and accepted results in records with named owners and a history of every change. See [From personal worker to business worker](../../../claude-and-chatgpt-2/architecture/from-personal-worker-to-business-worker.md).
- **12, containment or authority:** the threads were contained (they could not send). Mark was protected by Nisha's authority over what was sent.
- **13, a worker with its own badge:** Claude Tag is in public beta on Team and Enterprise plans. An Owner or a Claude Tag admin sets up its connections for a workspace or a channel, and Anthropic's security page for Tag covers audit trails. In a one-to-one message, Tag runs on the member's own Claude account, and in a channel it can use a member's own connectors for that member's requests, after the member allows it.[^tag] Projects are not yet offered on Team or Enterprise plans, so moving to a team plan for Claude Tag would cost Nisha her project setup. Even with Tag, nothing by itself gives her the client's business record or an enforced approval before anything is sent to Mark.

## Grading the lab (10 points)

Use the same rubric that is on the [lab page](../../../claude-and-chatgpt-2/case-2-nishas-first-client/lab-run-nishas-first-client-job.md#how-the-lab-is-graded).

| Points | What earns them |
|---|---|
| 2 | The brief: all four parts, a "Done when" sentence, and a "never send" rule a stranger could follow |
| 2 | The permission map and the lock test: every row marked locked or only asked, with the setting that locks it, and a recorded test showing the sending tools were not available. A screenshot alone earns no points for the test. |
| 1 | The version log: at least two dated versions, each with what changed and every assumption |
| 2 | The grading sheet: who caught each item on the answer key's list, in all four columns, with both lease answers shown |
| 1 | The acceptance checklist: every check marked, with the document that would close it, the label, and who may release the work |
| 2 | The reflection and reply: one decision for Thursday night, with its risk named and who would send it |

The [answer key](https://github.com/panaversity/agentfactory-v2-resources/releases/latest/download/nisha-lab-case-2-key-task-7.md) lists the eight items, the expected figures and how to mark the lock test and the checklist.

Give full points for honest records of what went wrong, such as a run that stopped or a lock that failed its test. Take points off for claims with no record behind them.

## The final assessment

After both cases, set the [final assessment](../../../claude-and-chatgpt-2/case-2-nishas-first-client/final-assessment-a-new-ai-worker.md). It gives learners a new job with no accounting and only made-up data, and asks them to plan an AI Worker on their own: what it may execute and what it may only draft, where state is saved, which permissions are locked and how they test the lock, and what evidence a person needs before approving. It tests whether the lessons transfer, not whether students can repeat Nisha's steps. Model answers and marking notes are in the [model answers](https://github.com/panaversity/agentfactory-v2-resources/releases/latest/download/nisha-lab-case-2-key-task-final-assessment.md) file. The final assessment links to it at its end, for learners who study alone.
