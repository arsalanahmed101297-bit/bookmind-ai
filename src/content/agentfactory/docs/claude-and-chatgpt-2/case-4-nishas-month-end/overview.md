*A job that took two days now takes about two hours of people's time. The firm did not buy a new tool. It joined the ones it already had.*

Panaversity case study · November 2026

## The case in short

A small accounting firm must prepare a report for a client at the end of every month. Done by hand, it takes a junior accountant two days: download the bank's lines, match them against the client's books, then type the result into a sheet, a memo and slides.

The firm's owner, Nisha, already uses Claude. She has always used its features one at a time. For this job she joins them into **one setup**: a project for the client's rules, a skill for the method, the files in the shared drive and in a folder on Hamza's computer, a program on a borrowed desk (a temporary computer in the cloud), four artifacts for the pack, and a scheduled task that looks at the bank each morning.

On Tuesday the report goes to the client, Dana. On Wednesday morning, something changes. What the firm does next is the second half of the story, and you meet it yourself in the lab first.

> **What this case teaches.** Each feature of Claude and ChatGPT is useful alone. Joined together, they become a workplace for a job that comes back every month. The setup reads, computes, builds and repeats. People check, approve and decide. In one line: **build the setup once, and it does the job again. Your judgment is still yours.**

> **Is this not just Google Docs with AI?** No. Writing was never the slow part of this job. [What the setup did](what-the-setup-did.md#is-this-better-than-a-document-tool) compares the two fairly.

## What you will be able to do

After this case you can:

1. choose the right part for each need, from a project for the rules to a scheduled task for the time;
2. join those parts into one setup for a job that repeats;
3. say what the setup does and what a person must still do: check the result, approve it by name, and tell the client;
4. decide what to change, and where, when something goes wrong after the work is approved.

## What you will do

- **Do the lab.** You build a smaller version of the setup with sample files: a project, a skill, one run that makes three pieces, and a late bank line. You can also add a sheet, a folder and a real scheduled task.
- **Decide.** At the end the client's owner wants the same work for four companies, a month-end pack and four weekly packs each, some sent straight to the bank, for less money. The case does not say what Nisha should answer.

> **What you need.** The Claude desktop app, on a Claude plan with projects, as the [lab](lab-build-the-month-end.md) says. Three optional steps add a sheet, a folder on your computer and a real scheduled task. You can do everything alone.

## You do not need the earlier cases

This is the fourth case about the same firm, but you can read it alone. Here is everything you need.

- **The firm.** A small accounting firm in Karachi. It pays for Claude's Team plan. **Nisha** owns it. **Sana** is the senior reviewer. She has fifteen years of experience checking companies' accounts, and works Monday to Thursday. **Hamza** is the junior accountant.
- **The client.** **Northgate Supply**, a company in Cleveland, Ohio, that sells kitchen and cleaning supplies to restaurants. **Dana Ruiz** is in charge of its accounts. **Walter Brennan** owns it. **Tom Becker** works at its bank.
- **The worker.** The firm's [AI Worker](../overview.md) is Claude, running inside Slack, its team chat. This case calls it **the worker**. The Claude that runs the month-end project is separate, and the case calls it **Claude**.
- **The firm's habit.** Before anything goes to a client, Nisha or Sana approves it, and the approval names the file and its figure. Of the three options [Case 3](../case-3-nishas-firm/mondays-decision.md#three-ways-to-release-the-file) ended with, the firm chose the first, a better rule, and kept this half of it. The other half, never send a file with an open item, it changed: such a file goes only when the approval also names what is open. It is still a rule, not a setting.
- **The time.** In October, Karachi is 9 hours ahead of Cleveland. When it is 6 p.m. in Karachi, it is 9 a.m. in Cleveland.

You do not need to know accounting. Each story page explains its new words in a box where they first appear.

This case's own word: the **setup** is the parts joined together for one job.

The story takes place in 2027, a little after this book was written.
