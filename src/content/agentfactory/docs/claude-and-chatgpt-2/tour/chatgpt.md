### One app for Chat, Work and Codex

OpenAI's new desktop app brings three ways of working into one app. It comes with every ChatGPT plan, including the free one. It runs on Mac and Windows, and OpenAI's download page now offers Linux too.[^openai-app],[^analytics-insight-classic],[^openai-desktop-app]

- **Chat** is everyday conversation. You ask, and ChatGPT answers.
- **Work** is for longer jobs. It is an **agent**, which means an AI that carries out a job in steps, instead of only answering. Work gathers information from your apps and files and splits a project into steps. It can work for hours if needed, and it returns finished materials.
- **Codex** is OpenAI's coding agent, with its own view and its own history.

In the app, you choose Chat or Work with a toggle above the message box. You switch to Codex separately. Chat and Work conversations share one list of recent chats. **Projects** keep related chats, files and instructions together, for both Chat and Work.[^openai-work-codex],[^openai-merge],[^lds-chat-work-merge]

ChatGPT has several **plans**, which are its price levels. Free, Go, Plus and Pro are plans for one person. Business, Enterprise and Edu are for companies, large organizations and schools. A **workspace** is a company's or school's shared ChatGPT account, with its own settings and administrators.

Since November 17, 2026, the newest models in Work and Codex are GPT-6.2 Sol and GPT-6.2 Astra. Chat shows GPT-6.[^openai-free-model] [Which model for which job](which-model.md) lists them all, and your plan and workspace settings decide which you see. Chat answers can also mix text with interactive charts, comparisons and small tools, such as a calculator, when that suits the question.[^openai-gpt62],[^openai-work-codex],[^openai-models],[^notebookcheck-astra],[^lds-gpt6],[^openai-release-notes]

### What Work can do

- **Work with your files and apps.** In the desktop app, Work can use files and apps on your computer. Work you start in the cloud keeps going on the web and on your phone.
- **Use a browser.** The desktop app has a built-in browser for research and web tools. On some websites, that browser lets ChatGPT use tools that the website itself provides. OpenAI calls these site tools, or **WebMCP**, after MCP, the standard most connectors use. Work can also use a **cloud browser**, which is a browser on a separate computer in the cloud. The cloud browser keeps its own sign-ins. So when a website asks you to log in, you log in again inside the cloud browser. The cloud browser never uses the passwords or open tabs in your own browser. Separately, the ChatGPT browser extension adds ChatGPT to the sidebar of Chrome and some other browsers.[^openai-browser],[^openai-desktop-app],[^openai-changelog]
- **Use your computer.** **Computer use** lets ChatGPT click, type and move files across your apps in the background. It can do this once, or as part of a scheduled task. On a Mac, Record & Replay lets you show ChatGPT a workflow once. ChatGPT then turns what you did into a **skill**, which is a saved way of doing a job that it can reuse.

### What Work makes and shares

- **Produce finished work.** Work makes documents, spreadsheets, presentations and web apps, often built to your templates and reference files. **Sites**, in beta, are interactive web apps such as dashboards, trackers and internal portals. You can share a site with your team or publicly. ChatGPT can also update a site as the information behind it changes.
- **Build decks with your team.** **Slides** makes a presentation from a conversation, a file, an outline or a template. It uses your brand style if you supply one. Ask "create a slide deck from this chat," and the deck opens in a slide editor. There you can change text, charts, tables and images yourself. Decks live in **Space**, which is ChatGPT's shared area for pages and decks. You invite people to view or edit a deck. Teammates can select part of a slide and ask ChatGPT to change it. You can present from ChatGPT with speaker notes. On the web, you can also export a copy to PowerPoint, PDF or Google Slides. The live deck stays in Space.[^openai-slides],[^openai-space-start],[^beri-slides]

### How Work connects, repeats and remembers

- **Use your apps.** **Plugins** connect ChatGPT to the tools you already use, such as Slack, Teams, Google Drive, SharePoint, email, calendars and CRMs. A **CRM** is the system a company uses to keep track of its customers. ChatGPT picks the right plugins itself, or you name one by typing "@" and its name.[^openai-app] ChatGPT calls its connectors plugins. In Claude, a plugin is a package of skills and connectors.
- **Repeat what worked.** Skills let ChatGPT follow the same method each time. The results still need checking. When you like how ChatGPT handled something, ask it to turn that into a skill. You can also describe a skill to the built-in skill creator, write one in an editor, or upload a skill file. ChatGPT uses an installed skill by itself when the skill fits the task. In a scheduled task in the desktop app, you can also call one by typing "$" and its name.[^openai-skills],[^openai-tasks],[^ai-career-lab-skills],[^claude-desktop-video],[^chatgpt-features-video]
- **Run on a schedule or on an event.** **Scheduled tasks** run once, on a schedule, or to watch for a change. They can also start when something happens in a connected app. That can be a new Gmail message, a new message in a Slack channel, or activity on a GitHub pull request. Plugins can also start a task with their own events, through MCP.[^openai-tasks],[^openai-scheduled-tasks],[^openai-release-notes],[^developers-digest-mcp-events]
- **Remember.** When you turn on **Memory**, ChatGPT can remember useful preferences and details from your chats. Depending on your plan, it can also use other sources, such as saved files and connected apps like Gmail.[^openai-memory]
- **Keep some chats private.** You can lock a chat behind a four-digit PIN. On a phone, you can also open it with Face ID, Touch ID or your device unlock. A locked chat disappears from your regular history and search. You find it under **Projects > Locked chats**. Locking hides a chat from people who use your device. However, it is not **encryption**, which would scramble the chat so that nobody without the key could read it. And if you forget your PIN, the only way to reset it deletes every locked chat.[^openai-locked],[^lds-locked-chats]

### Where ChatGPT's work actually runs

[Where the work happens](../new-way-of-working/where-the-work-happens.md) explains the main difference from Claude. OpenAI calls its cloud computers **sandboxes**. In the cloud, each one is backed by a virtual machine on OpenAI's servers. The saved work kept between tasks is called **state**.[^cloud-openai],[^openai-sandboxing] Here are the details.

- **Deleting is not immediate.** Ending a task or deleting a chat does not remove everything stored with it right away. Your workspace has **retention settings**, which decide how long saved data is kept. They also decide how long saved state is kept.
- **What it cannot reach.** The cloud environment does not get your local files, installed apps, browser sign-ins or company network.
- **Steps on your own computer.** A Work task can run some steps on your connected computer, which must be online. If it is offline, an existing task can continue in the cloud, but without your files.
- **Local work in the desktop app and Codex** runs on your own computer, not in a virtual machine. It runs inside a sandbox that your operating system provides. Your **permission mode** sets what that sandbox allows. A permission mode is a setting for what ChatGPT may do without asking you first. The section on permissions below explains the choices.

### Codex in the same app

Codex is to ChatGPT what [the Code tab](claude-code-tab.md) is to Claude. OpenAI says you can use it for research, documents, presentations and other knowledge work.[^codex-work] What Codex adds is developer views and technical detail. These matter most when software gets built, reviewed and tested.

In the desktop app, Codex can do these jobs:

- Edit a change directly in the view that shows it.
- Review pull requests in a side panel.
- Work across several repositories in one project.
- Give each chat its own copy of a repository, called a **worktree**, so jobs that run at the same time do not collide.
- Run several coding tasks at the same time in Codex Cloud, on OpenAI's computers.

**Goal mode** keeps Codex working toward one goal for hours or even days. It stops when Codex judges the goal complete, or when a **token budget** runs out. A token is a small piece of text, so a token budget is a limit on how much text the model may read and write. From your phone, Codex Remote lets you follow and steer a Codex session that runs on your computer. You find it in the Remote tab of the ChatGPT mobile app.[^codex],[^openai-desktop-app],[^openai-changelog],[^openai-work-codex],[^willison-codex-goals],[^gradually-codex-changelog]

### Permissions and governance

You control what ChatGPT can reach, when it checks in, and which actions need your approval. In the desktop app, you choose one of these permission modes.

- **Ask for approval** works inside the project folder you opened. It asks before it edits files outside that folder or uses the internet.
- **Approve for me** keeps the same folder limits. However, it sends approval requests to **auto-review** instead of to you. Auto-review is a reviewer model that checks each request. It asks you only about the actions it finds risky.
- **Full access** removes the limits on files and internet use.
- **Custom** applies rules from a settings file.

In the cloud browser, permission to visit a website is separate from permission to take a serious action on it, such as a purchase. In companies, administrators decide who has access, which tools ChatGPT can connect to and which actions it may take. They can also see Work conversations and actions through the **Compliance API**. In OpenAI's words, it gives visibility into Work "to support enterprise oversight", which means a company's own checks on how its people use AI.[^openai-perms],[^openai-cloud-browser],[^openai-permission-modes]

Each plan includes a **usage allowance**, which is the amount of work you can ask for before you wait for a reset or buy more. Complex tasks use more of it than quick questions. Work and Codex share one usage allowance.[^openai-usage],[^openai-work-codex],[^lds-chat-work-merge]

### Briefing ChatGPT

OpenAI's prompting advice for its recent models matches Anthropic's closely. State the goal, the relevant context, the constraints, the evidence required, the success criteria and the output format. State each instruction once. Say once which actions need your approval, such as sending, deleting or buying something. Repeating "ask first" makes the model stop even for safe steps. Start **reasoning effort**, OpenAI's name for effort, at medium, and raise it only when the gain is worth the time and cost. To shorten a prompt that works, remove one group of instructions at a time. Then rerun the same test tasks and compare the results.[^brief-openai],[^openai-prompt-guidance],[^openai-latest-model]

So the same brief works on both apps, and this book teaches briefing once, not once per app.
