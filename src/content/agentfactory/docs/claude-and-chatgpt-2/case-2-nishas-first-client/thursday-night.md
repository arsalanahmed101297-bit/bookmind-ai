## An email at seven

Nisha got home on Thursday at seven. There were two emails waiting.

The first was from Sutlej's accountant. The rent was Rs 10.0 million a year. The engagement letter had a typing error. The Rs 2.0 million difference in inventory was goods in transit (goods bought or sold but still on their way), and he would send the documents on Monday. The lease contract would come tomorrow.

The second was from Mark.

*"Thanks for yesterday's progress note. Very clear. Two things. First, please send me the final bridge by 6 p.m. New York time today, as agreed. Second, I hear you are using an AI system. From now on, could it email me its nightly update directly? Then I see progress every morning without waiting for you. If this works, I have five more targets to look at this quarter."*

6 p.m. in New York was 4 a.m. on Friday in Karachi. Nisha had the whole night. But the lease contract would not come tonight.

## What the lease line depended on

Version 2 showed both answers ([Exhibit 4](exhibits.md#exhibit-4-the-us-gaap-bridge)), and only the contract could settle them. It was a line she had not checked, about a contract she had not seen, going to a board she had never met, under her own name.

## Three drafts

Nisha asked Claude to help her draft three replies to Mark. She would choose one.

1. **Say yes to both.** Send the bridge tonight. Connect her email so the AI Worker could send Mark its nightly update directly from now on.
2. **Keep the second signature.** Send the inventory and development lines tonight, which she had checked. Tell Mark the lease line would follow on Monday, after she saw the contract. Keep sending every update herself.
3. **Send everything, with a note.** Send the full bridge tonight, with the lease line marked as based on an assumed operating lease and not yet checked, and the finance lease answer of Rs 102.0 million beside it. Say nothing yet about the nightly emails.

She thought about the first option. If she connected her email, the worker's update would arrive from her address, under her name. Mark would not be able to tell what she had read and what she had not.[^appears-as-you] She could add a line to the instructions, *"never send anything unchecked"*. She knew what such a line was worth.

She also thought about doing the nightly email from ChatGPT instead. ChatGPT's Work can run scheduled tasks and use an email plugin.[^chatgpt-work] But the email would still go out from her own account. Changing the tool did not change who the message came from.

She thought about the second. Mark would have most of his answer, and every number would be one she had checked. But he had asked for a bridge, and his board met in the morning. A gap in a first job might be the last job.

She thought about the third. Mark would have everything on time. The note would be honest. But would a board read a footnote?

## Before midnight

Nisha looked at the clock. It was 10:40. She had three drafts, one unchecked line, one client and five possible jobs. She had built a worker that could do the work while she slept. She had not built anything that could decide what to send.

She had until she went to bed to choose.
