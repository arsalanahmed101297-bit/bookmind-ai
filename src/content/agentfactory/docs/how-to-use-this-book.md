This book is written for four kinds of reader, and no reader needs all of it. Find your route in this chapter, read what it asks, and skip the rest until you need it.

> [!NOTE]
> **After this chapter, you will know:**
>
> - which route through the book fits you
> - how big your route is and what it costs
> - what you will have at the end of your route
> - how to learn with the labs, Appendix C and Zia Tutor AI

**Every reader starts with Claude and ChatGPT 2.0,** the first section in the sidebar. If you came to this page first, [start there](claude-and-chatgpt-2/overview.md).

**Then choose your route below.** Pick one area of your own work, and apply every exercise to that area. If you do not have one yet, use Brightline, the company you met in [One job, start to finish](claude-and-chatgpt-2/new-way-of-working/one-job.md). From Part I on, the book follows an AI Worker that does Brightline's accounts payable (AP) work: recording, checking and preparing the bills it pays.

## Four reader routes

Choose the route that matches your work today. You can change routes later.

On every route except the leader route, your first credential is PCAO-F, Panaversity's Associate certification. [Appendix C](appendices/exams/associate-foundations/overview.md#the-pcao-f-exams) describes its two exams, and [the roadmaps](certification-and-portfolio-roadmaps.md#how-to-read-an-exam-code) explain the exam codes and show every later exam.

| Route | What you read | Credential | Outcome: what you will have at the end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Everyday professional or manager | Claude and ChatGPT 2.0, the opening pages, Part 0, Parts I and II, and Appendix C | PCAO-F, then optionally CCAO-F, which has its own entry rules (see [Appendix C](appendices/exams/associate-foundations/overview.md#the-ccao-f-exam)) | One real task from your work, delegated to an AI Worker and checked, and a written review contract for it |
| Builder or engineer | Claude and ChatGPT 2.0, the opening pages, Part 0, and Parts I to III. Later parts continue the route | PCAO-F, PCDV-F, PCDV-P, then PCAR-F | A published AI Worker plugin (a package others can install), connected to approved knowledge and business data, with tests that measure how well it works |
| Domain expert or founder | Claude and ChatGPT 2.0, the opening pages, Part 0, Parts I and II, and your chapters in Part III. Later parts continue the route | PCAO-F | A written decision on which area of professional work to build for, and a first working piece of it, built with a builder |
| Enterprise or national leader | Claude and ChatGPT 2.0, the opening pages, Part 0 (optional), and Part I. Later parts continue the route | None required | A diagnostic of how AI-native your company is, or a plan for your country |

### How big each stage is

Your route is the stages it includes. The book gives no reading time or lab time, because a stage takes as long as your own work does. What it gives instead is the size of each stage, so you can plan.

| Stage | Who reads it | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Claude and ChatGPT 2.0](claude-and-chatgpt-2/overview.md) | Every route | Five sections and four cases, with a practice lab and a lab for each case |
| The Tour of the Apps and The Architecture, two optional sections of 2.0 | Anyone who wants them | Two sections to read, with no lab |
| The opening pages, from The Five Horizons to From Skills to Income | Every route | A few pages to read, with no lab |
| [Part 0](how-ai-works/overview.md) | Every route, but optional on the leader route | Four chapters, each with a lab |
| [Part I](ai-worker-paradigm/overview.md) | Every route | Four chapters, each with a lab |
| [Part II](managing-ai-workers/overview.md) | Every route except the leader route | Seven chapters, each with a lab |
| [Appendix C](appendices/exams/associate-foundations/overview.md) and its two sample exams | Readers who take PCAO-F or CCAO-F | The route to each exam, and a sample exam the size of each PCAO-F exam |
| [Part III](building-ai-workers/overview.md) | Builders, and domain experts by chapter status | Twelve chapters so far, of nineteen, each with a lab that is a full build |

The labs are where the time goes, because that is where you do the work. A lab can take several sittings, and on a free plan it can spread over several days. The builder route is the longest, because Part III's labs are full builds. The later parts are not written yet.

If you do not work at one, the only other way is Panaversity's FDE Internship Program, which also asks for PCAR-F, a builder exam, as [the roadmaps](certification-and-portfolio-roadmaps.md#the-certification-map) explain.

### If you are on the builder route

[Chapter 12](building-ai-workers/agentic-coding/overview.md) teaches you to drive a coding agent. Chapters [13](building-ai-workers/programming-fundamentals/overview.md) to [15](building-ai-workers/code-review/overview.md) cover programming fundamentals, program structure and code review. ### If you are still choosing

A few notes on choosing:

- **Not sure which route?** Start with the everyday professional route. Its reading, Parts I and II, is part of every other route except the leader route, so none of it is lost if you switch.
- **An accountant, lawyer or other specialist?** Take the everyday professional route if you want AI Workers to help with your own work. Take the domain-expert route if you want to build one for others in your field, together with a builder.
- **A founder with a technical partner?** Take the domain-expert route and read it beside your partner's builder route. A later part explains how the founding pair divides the work.

### What you need, and what it costs

The builder route builds software with a coding agent. On the other routes, you work with everyday AI products.

You can start the exercises and labs on the free plans of Claude[^claude-pricing] and ChatGPT.[^chatgpt-pricing] Both free plans were checked on 23 November 2026, and plans change often. Some later exercises need a paid subscription, and builders pay separately when their own software calls a model (API usage), on both Anthropic's[^anthropic-api-billing] and OpenAI's[^openai-api-billing] platforms. Each chapter's dated section on current tools says which plans its exercises need, and we review those sections every month. The open-source tools are free to use. Running them takes your own computer or server, and an account with a model provider unless you run a model yourself.

## Three journeys, one path

The book connects three journeys. Every reader develops capability. Certification and economic goals depend on the route you choose. This book keeps all three on one path, so you can follow as many as your route needs.

| Journey | The question it answers | Where it ends |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability | What can I actually do? | Depending on your route, you can manage, build, govern, compose or ship AI Workers |
| Certification | What has an independent exam verified? | PCxx credentials, then the corresponding Anthropic certifications |
| Economic | How does capability become income, and then national capacity? | Better work in your role, which can grow into Forward Deployed Engineer or founder work, an AI-Native Company or an AI-Native Country |

## If you are a domain expert

If you are an accountant, lawyer, doctor or engineer on the domain-expert route, you still read the technical parts. You read each one at the depth its chapter asks of you. An AI Worker in your field is only as good as the judgment you put into it. So every chapter in Parts III to V has a status for you, one of four things it asks of you. The "Who reads it" line in each part's "You are here" panel names the chapters for each one.

| What you do | What it asks of you |
| --- | --- |
| Read it in full | Understand the concept fully. |
| Read the summary | Know what it does and what questions to ask about it. You do not implement it. |
| Skip it if you like | Optional for you. |
| Co-build it | Supply the official rules, examples, edge cases and approval criteria while a builder implements. |

**A chapter you read as a summary opens with one for you,** under "For managers". It is about 500 words long. It covers what the concept does and the questions to ask a builder about it. Read the summary instead of the full chapter.

**Co-build chapters are where your expertise enters the system.** The running project in this book is Brightline's AP Worker. At each co-build step, you will see what the AP expert supplies beside what the builder implements. For example, the AP expert supplies approval thresholds, exception cases and sign-off criteria. Your version of that step uses your own field.

## Two vendors, open source, one set of principles

The book teaches two AI vendors, Anthropic and OpenAI, and open-source tools beside them. The [Preface](preface.md#the-thesis-and-five-lines) gives the reason. The book's three main open-source projects are:

- **OpenCode,** a coding agent that runs on your own computer.[^opencode-v2]
- **OpenClaw,** an assistant that keeps working while you are away.[^openclaw]
- **Omnara,** a runtime for running AI Workers on your own servers.[^omnara]

Open-source tools are the only route when a client's data must never leave its own systems, even for the model. Every chapter that shows one also says how to run it safely.

Other commercial model vendors are not taught or compared. Other companies appear only as systems a worker connects to, such as a messaging app or a client's accounting system.

Every technical topic follows the same seven steps: the principle, a dated box each for Anthropic, OpenAI and open source, the comparison (only the differences that change what you do), the invariant (what stays the same), then a lab.

In a chapter, steps 2 to 6 are one page about your own app. You pick the Claude, ChatGPT or Open source tab (steps 2 to 4), then read "What changes a step" (step 5) and "What stays the same" (step 6).

## Learning with Zia Tutor AI

Reading a concept is not the same as owning it. Zia Tutor AI closes that gap. It teaches one numbered concept at a time and checks that you have it before you move on. It is served from the same approved source as this book, so it teaches the same version of each concept. If the tutor and the book ever disagree, the book is the authority.

To use the tutor, add the Zia Tutor AI connector to your AI assistant. The book's website gives the current setup steps.

A session with the tutor follows a fixed rhythm:

1. What happens: a situation first, then the term that names it.
2. Why it works that way: the reason behind the behavior.
3. What it rules out: the wrong picture, named as wrong.
4. Say it back: you explain the concept in one line, in your own words.
5. Check: about four multiple-choice questions. The concept closes when you answer them with at most one miss.

If you cannot use the tutor, follow the same rhythm on your own. Read the numbered concept, explain it in one line in your own words, then answer its practice questions and build its artifact.

Work through each concept with the tutor after you read its chapter, and return to it for review before an exam.

The tutor checks understanding. It does not replace the labs. Capability comes from producing the artifact.
