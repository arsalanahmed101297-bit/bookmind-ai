**In everyday life.** You want 12 × 50, but you type 12 × 5 into a calculator. It shows 60, at once and with no doubt. The calculator did its job. Noticing that 60 cannot be right is your job. AI works the same way, on a much larger scale. It writes a full report in seconds, and it sounds sure. Deciding whether that report is right is still your job. It is now the most valuable part of the work.

**What you will learn.** One habit, the **checkpoint**. Before you act on anything AI produced, you stop and ask four questions:

1. What exactly is being claimed?
2. What is it based on?
3. What is being assumed?
4. What would change the conclusion?

Then you name who decides, and you write it down.

The lessons teach the skills that make it work.

**Why it matters.** A model sounds the same whether it is right or wrong ([Chapter 0.1](../what-ai-actually-is/why-it-behaves.md)), so the value is now in the checking. [Chapter 0.3](../ai-fluency/the-four-ds.md) named Discernment and Diligence. This chapter teaches the thinking underneath them.

## Which case does this chapter follow?

The chapter follows one memo at Brightline: an AI told Dave to pay every vendor early, and Ana asked for five minutes before he sent it on.

## What will you have at the end?

A one-page decision note built from your own decisions, with its numbers checked against the real invoice file. You will also have a habit for the rest of the book.
